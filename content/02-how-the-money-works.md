# How the money works

A student society does not have a bank account. It has a **fund** inside the University of Cape Town's accounting system, and every rand the society earns or spends passes through UCT's Finance Department. As treasurer you never handle cash and never make a payment yourself. You ask UCT to pay, and UCT pays.

That single fact explains almost everything else on this site.

## Your society, in four numbers

UCT runs its finances on SAP, a large accounting system. Inside it, your society is identified by a small set of numbers. You will write three of them on every purchase request.

| Number | What it identifies |
| --- | --- |
| **Fund number** | The pot of money that belongs to your society. Spending comes out of it and income goes into it. |
| **Cost centre** | The organisational unit the fund belongs to. For societies this sits under the Department of Student Affairs. |
| **GL account number** | The general ledger account, which classifies the type of expense (for example "student society activities") for UCT's books. |
| **Vendor number** | Not your society's number. Every supplier UCT is allowed to pay has one. You look it up in the vendor search on this site. |

The first three are specific to your society. Get them from the previous treasurer's handover, or from Student Treasury if the handover did not include them. Keep them somewhere private that the next treasurer will inherit, and never post them anywhere public. This site does not ask for them anywhere that is stored or sent.

## Who is in the chain

Four parties touch every transaction. Learn their roles now and the form will make sense later.

- **You, the treasurer.** You gather the quote, fill in the form, and keep the records. Nothing starts until you do.
- **The fund holder.** A staff member in the Department of Student Affairs who is responsible for every society fund. Nothing is paid without the fund holder's signature on the form. Forms go to a shared mailbox, societies.fundholder@uct.ac.za, where a small team checks them on the fund holder's behalf. This is the slowest step in the chain, so send complete paperwork the first time.
- **Student Treasury.** The office in the Department of Student Affairs that processes society purchases. They check your form, create the purchase order in SAP, and are the people you visit to check your balance.
- **UCT Finance.** The central departments behind Student Treasury. **Creditors** pays suppliers. **Debtors** invoices people who owe UCT money, such as sponsors.

## Checking the balance

There is no online view of your society's balance. You check it by going to Student Treasury in person. They sit in Room 5.10, Steve Biko Building, Upper Campus. Go before you plan an event budget, not after.

:::watch
Money that a sponsor has promised is not money in the account. A sponsor has only paid when Student Treasury confirms the amount has landed in the fund. Plan spending on what has landed, not on what is coming.
:::

## What the MM010 does

The MM010 turns "we want to buy this" into a **purchase order**, which is UCT's formal commitment to pay the vendor. The vendor delivers against the purchase order, invoices UCT, and UCT Creditors pays them. The money then comes out of your fund. Your job is to get the form right, get it signed, and keep the paperwork moving.
